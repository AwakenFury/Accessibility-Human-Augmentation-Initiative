The **Accessibility & Human Augmentation Initiative** explores how wearable computing, embedded artificial intelligence, biosignal interfaces, environmental sensing, and secure cyber-physical systems can support accessibility, safety, and augmentation.

The initiative initially focuses on assistive technologies for users with achromatopsia, photophobia, low vision, and other sensory-accessibility needs. Rather than relying on color alone, the system emphasizes multimodal awareness, adaptive feedback, and user control.

Future Cyber-Bio Biowearables may integrate cameras, light sensors, inertial sensors, proximity detection, biosignal monitoring, and edge-computing hardware. These devices are intended to process environmental context in ways that improve situational awareness while preserving privacy and user autonomy.

With the user's explicit authorization, a Biowearable may also operate as an additional encrypted sensor feed for the Cyber-Bio Security platform. This connection could support personal safety, detection of hostile or unsafe conditions, and other contextual awareness use cases.

The wearer remains the primary authority over the device. Camera access, data transmission, retention, and third-party visibility should remain transparent, revocable, purpose-limited, and auditable.

## Core Principles

* Accessibility without dependence on color
* Human control over sensing and data sharing
* Local-first artificial intelligence
* Privacy-preserving environmental interpretation
* Secure and authenticated wearable devices
* Multimodal feedback through vision, audio, and haptics
* Transparent recording and transmission indicators
* Human-in-the-loop safety decisions
* Research-oriented and non-medical experimentation
