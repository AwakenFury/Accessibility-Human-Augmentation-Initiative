# Accessibility & Human Augmentation Initiative

The **Accessibility & Human Augmentation Initiative** explores how wearable computing, embedded artificial intelligence, biosignal interfaces, environmental sensing, and secure cyber-physical systems can enhance a person's ability to perceive and interact with their surroundings.

The initiative initially focuses on assistive technologies for users with achromatopsia, photophobia, low vision, and other sensory-accessibility needs. Rather than relying on color alone, the system converts environmental information into high-contrast labels, symbols, patterns, audio guidance, and haptic feedback.

Future Cyber-Bio Biowearables may integrate cameras, light sensors, inertial sensors, proximity detection, biosignal monitoring, and edge-computing hardware. These devices are intended to process accessibility information locally whenever possible.

With the user's explicit authorization, a Biowearable may also operate as an additional encrypted sensor feed for the Cyber-Bio Security platform. This connection could support personal safety, device-health monitoring, anomaly detection, emergency assistance, and controlled forensic event capture.

The wearer remains the primary authority over the device. Camera access, data transmission, retention, and third-party visibility should remain transparent, revocable, purpose-limited, and auditable.

## Core Principles

* Accessibility without dependence on color
* Human control over sensing and data sharing
* Local-first artificial intelligence
* Privacy-preserving environmental interpretation
* Secure and authenticated wearable devices
* Multimodal feedback through vision, audio, and haptics
* Transparent recording and transmission indicators
* Human-in-the-loop safety decisions
* Research-oriented and non-medical experimentation
